## 1. Why would a client buy Beam instead of SAP / Oracle / ServiceNow / Workday AI?

Those products automate work **inside their own object model**. Clients still
have email, shared drives, legacy ERPs, SMS, country-specific portals, and
exceptions the incumbent assistant cannot close.

Beam is the intelligence layer that runs the **process across systems**, with
a graph that must start and finish, evals on the steps that matter, and an
audit log. The user stays in the screen they already live in. Beam writes
back.

Use this when the process spans two or more systems, is exception-heavy, or
sits on a 15–30 year ERP the client will not rip out. Do not pick a fight
inside a clean, single-module S/4 workflow the ERP already does well.

## 2. How is this different from Anaplan / OneStream / other EPM?

EPM is a planning and data-model layer: one source of truth, forecasts,
close. Beam does not try to be that database.

If the client’s actual problem is "we have no planning model", that is an EPM
deal. If the problem is "70 people in a room judging invoices / orders /
claims because the ERP’s three-way match dies on exceptions", that is a Beam
deal. They can coexist. Beam pulls what it needs, acts, and does not keep a
second warehouse by default.

## 3. You do not store the data. So how do you work when the region’s mess is the data layer?

Honestly: we integrate. Custom integration builder (OAuth, API key, OpenAPI),
plus a large out-of-the-box catalog. We pull, decide if it is actionable, act,
and discard what we do not need.

We are not a process-mining suite and not a data-lake vendor. If the client
has no API and no export path, say so early. The first workflow should be the
one we can reach.

## 4. What does deployment look like in Saudi Arabia or Kuwait?

Do not state regional residency, on-prem, private-networking, or government
readiness as a generic promise. Use the current workspace-approved security
one-pager and loop Beam before a government or regulated-data claim. The
portal should host that proof next to this FAQ; if it is not approved, the
answer is “request Beam for a deployment review.”

## 5. Every GM already bought a small AI tool. Why will this not be another one?

Because we do not sprinkle assistants on unchanged processes. We pick **one
high-volume back-office chain**, agree success criteria, put it into
production with evals and exception paths, and only then expand.

Local tool sprawl with zero upside is the default. The counter is a
process-bank, a ranked first wave, and fees that can wait on proof.

## 6. Why Beam instead of us just building this ourselves / using a Big Four platform team?

PwC (and peers) already tried to productize this class of technology and
know how that fails: priorities, not talent. Beam’s job is the platform, the
agent factory, and the production eval/learning loop. The partner’s job is
the client, the operating model, the humans in the delivery center, and the
transformation story.

The useful sentence internally: we are not a rent-a-brand for a startup, and
Beam is not a staff-aug shop hiding in a partner logo. It is a packaged
motion.

## 7. Do we expose the Beam brand?

Three legal shapes, pick per client:

| Shape | When | What the client sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Partner-fronted / powered by Beam** | Brand-sensitive, managed services, BPO wrap | PwC (or BIT) product, Beam in the stack |
| **Joint** | Client wants to see the technology vendor | Both logos, Beam in the room for the demo |
| **Beam-fronted, partner-introduced** | Technical buyer, or Beam already has the logo | Beam product, partner on process and change |

Mo already offered PwC the first shape for shared services. Do not invent a
fourth shape on the call.

## 8. Are you also talking to Deloitte / EY / McKinsey in this region?

Partners will ask. Do not freelance a coverage map. The portal should carry
the current, approved "how we work with consulting firms" note: we do work
with other firms in other motions (example historically used: RPA-style
partners in Europe), and a named regional exclusive is a commercial
negotiation, not a slide.

Until that note is approved, the live answer is: we understand brand risk,
and the working proposal is a PwC-fronted shared-services motion rather than
a two-brand circus.

## 9. What do you want from us, and what do we get?

Beam wants distribution into rooms we cannot fill, process expertise, and
delivery-center capacity. The partner wants a credible automation/AI product
inside an existing managed-services or transformation offer, without having
to become a software firm.

Concrete first trade, as agreed on the PwC call: **one client, one workflow,
shadow environment, then a playbook.** Not a JV. Not a global MSA.

## 10. How do we make money?

Keep this high-level in the FAQ; put numbers only in a staff-approved
commercial annex.

Typical shapes already used with partners: co-sell, reseller / white-label,
co-delivery with Beam FDEs on assignment, outcome-tied client fees, then
license. BIT taught us that JV-on-the-core-product is how conversations die.

## 11. What does the first engagement look like?

1. Qualify the account (ICP, urgency, access). Walk away when it is an EPM
   or a single-module ERP assistant.
2. Outside-in view + process interviews (Iris / diagnostic).
3. Ranked first wave on the graph. Agree success criteria.
4. Shadow or sandbox workflow on one process with test data.
5. Production with evals, exception paths, audit log.
6. Expand along the value chain. License after value is visible.

Partners should be able to narrate this without opening a 40-slide deck.

## 12. What if we are an audit firm and independence is messy?

Flag early. The portal checklist: named client, service line, whether Beam
would be in the attest perimeter, who contracts, who hosts data, whether
white-label reduces conflict optics. Beam does not give legal advice. PwC
risk must clear the motion. The product can still be useful as a
pre-packaged description of roles so that conversation is not invented in
email.

## 13. Can we see a working agent, not a slide?

Yes. Request a vertical demo (order-to-cash, invoice exceptions, screening)
and, for the first joint client, a shadow workflow on **their** process.
Jack’s Fraisa-style demo is the pattern: inbox → classify → exceptions →
ERP write-back → audit agent.

Do not promise a same-day custom demo on an unscoped process.

## 14. How do you keep accuracy from decaying?

Production feedback (thumbs, failed code nodes, low evals) becomes training
data. Problems are clustered. One cluster is trained at a time, then
regression-tested against the others. That is the difference between a prompt
tweak and an agent that stays in production.
